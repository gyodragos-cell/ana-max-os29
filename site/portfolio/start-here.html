OS29 · Explained without technical jargon

What if your AI could pick up where it left off?

You ask an AI to fix a problem. It investigates for an hour. Then the session ends, or another AI takes over. Do you really want to explain everything again?

OS29 is a local notebook for AI agents: what was tried, what worked, what failed, and what should happen next. It is not another AI model.

Did this actually happen?

Yes. In a private local experiment, GPT-6 found a genuine mathematical bug. Qwen 7B independently retrieved the task and generated a partial repair. GPT-6 corrected one expression and ran independent tests. The details below describe that test, not the static illustration above.

BEFORE
5 failed

Wrong ranking. A score named “cosine” reached an impossible value of 20.

AFTER
7 passed

Correct ranking. The full private OS29 regression also passed 433 tests at that checkpoint.

Important: what is demonstrated, and what is not

Qwen did not repair the bug completely on its own. GPT-6 corrected one expression. This shows how agents can share a task, retain failed attempts, and verify a combined repair. The public demo uses invented data; it does not run two AI models in your browser. Zero bugs and viral success cannot be guaranteed.

Three words, translated into everyday language

AI agent

An AI assistant that can use tools to carry out steps, not just answer in a chat.

Work Unit

A task card containing the goal, progress, files, evidence and the next action.

Receipt / evidence

A record of what ran and the actual test result, so someone else can check it.
